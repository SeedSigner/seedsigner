# Dice grid scanner

The dice grid scanner creates a 24-word seed from one photo of 100 dice, instead of entering 99 rolls one at a time.

The rolls it reads go through exactly the same seed generation as manually entered rolls, so the result can be checked with the steps in [Verifying dice seed generation](dice_verification.md).


## What you need

* 100 standard 16 mm six-sided dice with pips (not numerals). The frame allows 2 mm of slack across the whole block.
* A printed dice grid sheet (see below).
* Even light, without strong shadows or glare across the dice.


## Print the sheet

The sheet is generated by `tools/dice_grid_sheet.py`:

```bash
pip3 install -e .
cd tools
python3 dice_grid_sheet.py dice_grid_letter.pdf --paper letter
python3 dice_grid_sheet.py dice_grid_a4.pdf --paper a4
```

Print at 100% / "Actual size" (not "Fit to page") and check the 100 mm bar on the sheet with a ruler.

The sheet has a square frame for the dice and four corner markers. The camera uses the markers to locate the frame, so all four must be visible and uncovered in the photo.


## Advice on obtaining and rolling/packing dice

Packs of 100 dice of 16mm are inexpensive and widely available.

This approach works well for rolling and arranging the dice:

1. Put the template on a clipboard.
1. Put the clipboard in a shallow box jammed into one corner.
1. Roll the dice so they stay on the clipboard.
1. Slide any dice that are on top of other dice off onto the clipboard, so all dice are in one layer.
1. Hold the box at an angle toward the corner where the clipboard is. Shake the box so that they tend to pack themselves into the corner. Shake from both side directions to best resolve dice that are stuck in a diagonal orientation.
1. Arrange the remaining dice so they form a 10x10 grid.
1. Remove the clipboard from the box with the dice in place.
1. Use a book or ruler to slide the whole block in each direction until the block lies within the grid square on the template.

## Scan

1. Roll the dice and push them into the frame as a packed 10x10 block, without looking at or rearranging them by value. Every die should sit flat.
2. On the SeedSigner: **Tools** > **New seed** (dice icon) > **24 words (scan grid)**.
3. Hold the SeedSigner above the sheet so the whole sheet, including all four markers, fills the preview. Hold it as level as you can and press the joystick to capture.
4. Review the rolls. Every roll is shown in grid order (row by row, top-left to bottom-right) for you to check against the real dice. Rolls the reader was unsure of are highlighted, and the review starts on the first of them. Click any roll to correct it.
5. Confirm. The first 99 rolls become the seed; the 100th die is not used.

If the markers can't be found, the SeedSigner asks you to retake the photo. If a die can't be read at all, you must enter its value before continuing.


## How it reads the dice

All processing happens on the SeedSigner using only numpy and Pillow; see `src/seedsigner/helpers/dice_grid_reader.py`. In short:

1. Find the four ArUco corner markers and recover the camera's position over the sheet.
2. Project the frame onto the plane of the dice tops, which sit one die height above the paper.
3. Fit a 10x10 lattice to the dark seams between the dice, then align each cell with its own die.
4. Read each die by matching its pips against the six possible pip patterns. Dark pips on light dice and light pips on dark dice both work.

The photo is held in memory only long enough to be read and is never saved. The rolls are cleared once the seed is created or the scan is abandoned.


## Verifying

Write down the 99 rolls from the review screen (or enter the same dice manually with **24 words (99 rolls)**) and follow [Verifying dice seed generation](dice_verification.md). Both paths must produce the same 24 words.
